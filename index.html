03 / THE LIVING LAND

A sanctuary
is something
you tend.

Since 2002, Rashani has stewarded Kipukamaluhia in the Kaʻū district of Hawaiʻi Island. The land is a place of shared work, individual and group retreat, biodiversity, and direct connection with the earth.

In dialogue with a friend and teacher

Rashani's relationship with Joanna Macy is part of her artistic and contemplative story. In a journal dedicated to Macy and the Great Turning, her collages accompany words and reflections on care for the world.

The project holds an especially personal place in her wider archive of tributes to the people who helped shape her work.

06 / RASHANI'S STORY

Art, activism
& a pathless life.

Rashani began writing poetry and studying brush painting at eight. Her path has included social and ecological activism, music, contemplative practice, creative collaborations, and decades of bringing people together.

Her sanctuary and her artwork aren't separate occupations; both reflect a sustained relationship with beauty, impermanence and the world around her.
